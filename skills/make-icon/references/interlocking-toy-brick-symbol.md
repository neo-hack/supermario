# Interlocking Toy-Brick Symbol

Use this reference when an app icon should express one unified symbol through a small assembly of modular toy-brick forms. Keep the construction graphic and iconic; do not render a branded construction toy or a realistic product photograph.

## Style DNA

- **Structure:** one compact assembly built from `3–7` visually large modules. The modules must resolve into one silhouette, not a pile of separate pieces.
- **Geometry:** block, plate, corner, step, arch, and short beam modules with a consistent grid and compatible proportions.
- **View:** a stable isometric or near-isometric view showing at most three face directions throughout the assembly.
- **Identity:** derive the symbol from stacking, stepping, enclosing, bridging, interlocking, or carving negative space.
- **Finish:** a flat isometric illustration with crisp, outline-free boundaries and only minimally rounded outer corners. Preserve the modular construction without simulating manufactured plastic.
- **Depth:** use two or three broad color levels assigned by face direction. Depth must come from geometry and clean color blocking, not lighting effects.
- **Composition:** center one upright, self-supporting assembly with a strong outer contour and a small number of deliberate internal gaps.

## Prompt Keywords

`modular toy-brick symbol`, `compact interlocking assembly`, `flat isometric illustration`, `large geometric modules`, `stepped construction`, `enclosed negative space`, `two or three flat color levels`, `minimal visible studs`, `no material rendering`, `32 px silhouette clarity`

## Symbol Contract

- Start with the intended silhouette or functional metaphor, then express it using the fewest compatible modules.
- Prefer one dominant mass with one secondary step, opening, bridge, or inset.
- Treat negative space as part of the mark. Use no more than two internal openings, and keep each large enough to survive at `32 × 32`.
- Avoid a uniform cube cluster with no hierarchy. At least one height, inset, bridge, or enclosure relationship must clarify the symbol.
- Do not use loose pieces, exploded diagrams, construction instructions, or a scene around the assembly.

## Module Budget

- Default to `4–6` modules; allow `3` for an exceptionally simple mark and `7` only when the silhouette remains clear at small size.
- A module should occupy at least `10%` of the assembly's visible area. Merge or remove smaller fragments.
- Show `3–6` studs across the entire assembly by default, never one stud on every possible grid position.
- Keep no more than `3` primary seams or junction lines visible at thumbnail size.
- Studs are structural cues, not decoration. Omit hidden, redundant, cropped, or visually noisy studs.

## Connection Contract

- Place every module on one shared orthogonal construction grid. Parallel edges must remain parallel and module widths, heights, studs, and offsets must resolve to the same base unit.
- Connect modules only through a complete shared rectangular face or a clearly centered stud-to-underside attachment. Never let one module disappear partway into another solid face.
- Every raised module must sit directly on a visible supporting module. A bridge is allowed only when both ends land cleanly on equal-height supports.
- Use flush contact by default: no hairline gaps, accidental overlaps, partial-face intersections, floating corners, or unsupported overhangs.
- Draw a seam only on the visible boundary between two complete modules. The seam follows the shared grid edge and stops cleanly where another module occludes it.
- At each junction, a viewer should be able to identify which module is in front, which is behind, and which supports the upper piece. If that order is ambiguous, simplify the junction or merge the forms.

## Surface and Depth Contract

- Assign each visible face direction one consistent color role: light top, medium side, dark side. Keep these as discrete graphic regions.
- Prefer solid fills. A face may use one barely perceptible monotonic gradient only when it remains visually flat and introduces no hotspot, rim, reflection, or rounded-volume cue.
- Render each stud as two simple regions: one flat top and one darker side. Add no bevel ring, edge highlight, or glossy cap.
- Allow one small offset grounding-shadow shape beneath the unified assembly. Keep it low contrast and compact; it must not extend farther than `6%` of the canvas width beyond the silhouette and must not create a halo.
- Use no cast shadows between distant pieces; express overlap with clean occlusion and face-value changes.

## Color Contract

- Choose a compact palette appropriate to the subject: one dominant color, one optional accent, and neutral support when needed.
- Preserve clear value separation among the three face directions and between adjacent modules.
- Do not default to any named hue. Vivid, pastel, muted, monochrome, analogous, or restrained complementary palettes are all valid.
- Limit the assembly to `2–4` perceived colors after accounting for face shading. More colors require a user-provided reason.

## Background Contract

- Default to a plain solid rounded-square base with clear contrast against the assembly.
- The base is support, not scenery. Add no room, tabletop, particles, grid, instruction sheet, or decorative brick fragments.
- If a broad background gradient is used, keep it nearly imperceptible and uniform across the canvas. Never align it to a module, accent color, or imagined light source.

## Proportion Contract

- Measure the complete assembly by its bounding box, including studs but excluding the grounding shadow.
- Default width and height are each `64–78%` of the canvas.
- Keep at least `11%` clear canvas on every side; no module or stud may touch the icon boundary.
- Keep the assembly's visual center within `5%` of the canvas center.
- Default overall aspect ratio is between `0.82:1` and `1.22:1`. A deliberately directional symbol may extend to `0.72:1` or `1.38:1`.
- Keep the tallest visible stack below `2.2×` the height of the shortest major mass so the assembly does not become a thin tower.

## Brand Boundary

- Use generic terms such as `interlocking toy brick`, `construction block`, `stud`, and `modular assembly`.
- Never reproduce a brand wordmark, embossed lettering, logo, minifigure identity, packaging, instruction graphics, or proprietary set design.
- Stud tops remain blank and featureless.

## Keep Out

Avoid realistic ABS plastic, product photography, glossy specular reflections, ray-traced lighting, rim light, bloom, glow, colored atmosphere, background haze, vignettes, deep ambient occlusion, complex bevels, edge highlights, surface texture, grain, micro-scratches, fingerprints, molded serial details, one stud per grid cell, dense seams, more than seven modules, scattered pieces, disconnected clusters, mismatched grid units, interpenetrating blocks, T-junctions entering solid faces, partial overlaps, hairline gaps, floating modules, unsupported bridges, voxel art, faceted low-poly treatment, glass, transparency, text, logos, watermarks, scenery, and hard dramatic shadows.
