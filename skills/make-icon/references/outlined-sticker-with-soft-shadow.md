# Outlined Sticker with Soft Shadow

Use this reference for a light, playful icon whose identity comes from one crisp sticker-like foreground mark.

## Style DNA

- **Structure:** one dominant sticker mark over a subordinate full-bleed color field.
- **Shape:** one compact continuous silhouette, one or two broad identity structures, and a few subordinate marks or strokes. Soft corners and broad internal spacing carry recognition.
- **Edge:** one thin-to-medium separation keyline around the outer silhouette. Add an inner contour only when the foreground would otherwise merge with the base.
- **Depth:** one close, low-contrast shadow separates the complete sticker from the base. The foreground remains flat.
- **Color:** assign clear palette roles, then choose hues from the subject, product mood, and required contrast. No hue family is the default.
- **Composition:** centered or optically offset mark occupying roughly 50–68% of the canvas with generous safe area.

## Prompt Keywords

`minimal outlined sticker character`, `single continuous silhouette`, `one or two broad identity structures`, `thin-to-medium separation keyline`, `optional inner contour`, `shallow diffuse sticker shadow`, `compact friendly proportions`, `simple full-bleed color field`, `context-aware palette`, `small-size clarity`

## Required Hierarchy

1. The sticker silhouette carries the identity; the outer keyline only separates it from the base.
2. When needed, one soft shadow provides separation without becoming a material effect.
3. The background supports contrast and may remain a plain solid or restrained gradient.

## Complexity Contract

- Build the subject from roughly `5–8` large filled shapes; the keyline and shadow do not expand this budget.
- Retain one or two subject-defining structures. Merge weaker cues into the silhouette or delete them.
- Use at most two broad internal identity regions. Do not subdivide them with construction lines.
- Allow up to three internal strokes when each one carries identity, expression, or necessary separation.
- Internal strokes are thick, rounded, short, and widely spaced. They share one visual weight and one palette role.
- Express remaining secondary structure through the silhouette or filled color regions, not additional explanatory linework.
- Prefer a frontal, side, or gentle three-quarter view. Keep perspective shallow and readable rather than diagrammatic.
- Test the black silhouette and the complete mark at `32 × 32`; delete any line or shape that does not survive.

**Sticker test:** mentally remove the keyline and shadow. The remaining subject should still read as a compact character mark with recognizable large structures, not a detailed product rendering.

## Simplification Order

When the result is busy or stiff, revise in this order:

1. Delete repeated controls, decorative strokes, and lines without a clear identity or separation role.
2. Delete identity cues beyond the strongest two.
3. Merge adjacent filled regions while preserving large structural relationships.
4. Reduce perspective depth without removing the pose.
5. Adjust expression and proportion only after the silhouette is clean.

Create warmth with mild asymmetry, imperfect curves, crop, spacing, or whole-mark tilt. Never create warmth by adding detail.

## Shadow Contract

- Use zero or one shadow for the complete sticker silhouette. Do not shadow individual internal regions.
- Keep it close to the keyline, with a small consistent offset in one direction.
- Use a broad, feathered edge and very low contrast against the base; the shadow should nearly disappear at `32 × 32`.
- Derive its hue from the base or contour instead of defaulting to neutral black.
- Keep the shadow narrower than the sticker keyline-to-canvas margin; it must not form a halo around the composition.
- The shadow only separates layers. It does not model volume, create lighting, or decorate the background.

Avoid glow, bloom, radial aura, ambient spotlight, multiple shadows, long cast shadows, hard-edged shadows, inner shadows, contact shadows inside the subject, and colored light spill.

## Palette Selection

Choose colors by role rather than by a preset swatch:

- **Base:** supports the product mood and separates from the sticker silhouette.
- **Foreground:** carries the subject identity; it may be light, dark, neutral, or chromatic.
- **Keyline:** creates immediate separation from the base; it need not be white.
- **Inner contour:** harmonizes with either the foreground or base while remaining clearly visible.
- **Internal strokes:** reuse the inner-contour or facial-mark color rather than introducing a new color family.
- **Shadow:** derives from the base or contour and stays low-contrast.

Prefer a compact palette of three to five semantic color families. Use analogous, complementary, warm-neutral, cool-neutral, muted, or vivid relationships when they fit the subject. Do not inherit colors from earlier examples unless the user asks for continuity.

### Near-White Filled Variant

Use this variant when the desired mark has a white, warm-white, or lightly tinted near-white body over a chromatic full-bleed base:

- Keep the near-white area as a solid foreground fill, not an empty canvas or transparent cutout.
- Use one chromatic rounded contour to define the subject. Add a pale outer separation rim only when contrast requires it; it must not become a decorative second outline.
- Choose the base and contour hues from the subject, product mood, and contrast needs. Blue is only one possible palette.
- Keep the foreground mostly flat, with at most slight tonal softness and the same single close shadow allowed by the Shadow Contract.
- Preserve the general shape and line budgets: roughly `4–7` large rounded shapes are sufficient for this especially minimal treatment, with at most three short internal strokes.

Treat requests such as “white base,” “white background,” or “白底” as ambiguous. When a supplied reference shows a colored canvas with a white subject, interpret white as the **subject fill**. Without resolving visual or conversational context, ask whether the user means a white canvas or a white-filled subject. Do not produce a colored line-only subject floating on white when this variant is selected.

## Optional Background Accent

Use at most one low-contrast treatment: a soft local highlight, faint haze, broad tonal patch, or subtle same-hue gradient. The accent stays behind the sticker and must not read as a scene or object.

## Adjustable Axes

- Contour: single separation keyline by default; optional inner contour only for contrast.
- Foreground: flat, lightly translucent, or softly filled.
- Foreground treatment: context-aware color fill, or the near-white filled variant over a chromatic base.
- Shadow: close and faint, minimally offset, or absent when base contrast is already sufficient.
- Palette: light-on-dark, dark-on-light, warm, cool, neutral, analogous, or complementary.
- Mood: airy, cheerful, dreamy, calm, or energetic.

## Keep Out

Avoid bubbles, droplets, floating spheres, literal glass objects, scenery, tactile paper grain, heavy clay volume, hard cast shadows, photoreal surfaces, thin line art, bevel-heavy emblems, perspective-driven detail, dense construction lines, repeated controls, mixed stroke weights, and multiple competing marks.
