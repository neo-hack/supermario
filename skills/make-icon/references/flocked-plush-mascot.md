# Flocked Plush Mascot

Use this reference only for a tactile full-bleed abstract mascot covered in dense ultra-short fur. Do not use it for recognizable objects, products, devices, animals, or scenes. The icon should feel softly flocked, not sewn, knitted, shaggy, or molded from clay.

## Style DNA

- **Structure:** one dominant foreground form and, optionally, one subordinate backing form. Use only two or three broad identity structures.
- **Shape:** compact, blunt, rounded, and softly asymmetric, with no fragile protrusions.
- **Surface:** dense uniform micro-fur follows the underlying curvature and gathers subtly around recesses and overlaps.
- **Face:** two simple eyes by default, formed as contrasting velvet-like indentations or clean recessed apertures. A tiny mouth is optional. Facial marks need not be black.
- **Depth:** rounded volume, shallow recesses, compressed seams, close overlap, and soft contact occlusion.
- **Composition:** full-bleed by contract; the result must read as an app icon rather than a product floating on a backdrop.

## Prompt Keywords

`full-bleed flocked plush icon`, `abstract soft mascot`, `dense ultra-short fur`, `micro-fiber surface`, `soft rounded macro-form`, `recessed velvet facial marks`, `context-aware colorful palette`, `shallow plush layering`, `soft studio light`, `fuzzy rim separation`, `small-size readability`

## Full-Bleed Composition Contract

- Fill the entire square with either the backing form or a simple supporting color field; never surround the subject with broad empty margins.
- Let the backing form or icon base occupy `94–100%` of the canvas and reach all four edges.
- Let the dominant foreground form occupy roughly `76–92%` of the canvas. It may crop slightly on one or two edges when that strengthens the icon.
- Keep the face and strongest identity structures inside the central `64%` safe region even when outer fur or backing layers crop.
- Use at most `4–10%` visible breathing room around the dominant silhouette. More space creates a product render rather than an icon.
- Do not add a floor, horizon, pedestal, or detached grounding shadow. Depth comes from overlap and tight occlusion inside the icon.

## Fur Contract

- Use a visually short `1–2 mm` pile with high, even density. The sculpted form remains readable beneath it.
- Fibers follow surface normals and change direction smoothly across curves.
- Compress fur slightly at recess edges, seams, and contact zones.
- Keep the outer silhouette softly fuzzy but stable at `32 × 32`.
- Use color and value changes between layers; do not rely on different fur lengths.

Avoid long hair, flyaway tufts, shag, curls, bald patches, random noise, felt sheets, woven fabric, visible stitching, knitted loops, plastic shine, or animal-like coat patterns.

## Subject Contract

- Use one compact regular or broadly organic abstract shape with one optional backing layer.
- Build identity from silhouette, proportion, overlap, face placement, and color rhythm rather than object parts.
- Use no product controls, screens, handles, apertures, mechanical grooves, architectural features, clothing, limbs, or anatomy.
- Test the silhouette without fur and at `32 × 32`; if the mascot loses its personality, simplify the form before adding texture.

## Lighting and Palette

- Use one broad soft key from above-left, gentle fill, restrained fuzzy rim separation, and tight ambient occlusion between layers.
- Keep shadows short, soft, and internal to the icon. Avoid glow, bloom, hard cast shadows, and dramatic spotlights.
- Assign three or four color roles: foreground mascot, backing form or base, facial marks, and optional compact accent.
- Choose hues from the requested mood, brand, subject personality, or surrounding product context. No hue family, saturation level, or neutral palette is the default.
- Vivid, pastel, muted, earthy, monochrome, analogous, or restrained complementary palettes are all valid when value contrast remains clear.
- Facial marks use the darkest or lightest suitable palette color; do not default to black when a deep chromatic color works.
- A micro-dot base is optional and may be chromatic, light, or dark. It must remain mostly hidden behind the full-bleed forms.
- Do not inherit gray, cream, black, sage, or any other colors from examples unless the user requests continuity.

## Keep Out

Avoid concrete objects, products, devices, tools, vehicles, furniture, scenery, wide empty borders, isolated product-shot composition, transparent cutout presentation, automatic grayscale or black-and-white palettes, photoreal animal fur, long plush fibers, glossy eyes, hard outlines, sticker borders, clay, foam, plastic, metal, detailed anatomy, limbs, accessories, text, logos, multiple characters, and dense controls.
